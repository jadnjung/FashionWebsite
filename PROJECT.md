# Esque — Product Requirements & Experience Document

Status: Initial Product Definition
Launch: Collection 001
Market: United States
Language: English
Commerce Model: Limited drop-based fashion
Initial Catalog: 6 products
Primary Experience: Desktop + mobile web
Future: Mobile application, expanded exclusivity system, Lookbook/editorial ecosystem

## 1. Product Vision

Esque is a niche, experimental fashion house built around limited clothing drops rather than a permanent traditional catalog.

The name Esque comes from the suffix meaning "like," "resembling," or "in the style or manner of." The name represents the brand's ability to explore different aesthetics, styles, and creative identities while remaining recognizably Esque.

Each drop may represent a different direction:

- avant-garde
- minimal
- streetwear
- contemporary
- experimental combinations of these styles

Esque should feel less like a conventional online clothing store and more like entering a digital fashion house.

The website itself is part of the Esque product.

The desired reaction is:

> "I want to come back."

and:

> "This feels like a fashion house."

The interface should therefore create curiosity and memorability without sacrificing usability.

## 3. Brand Personality

Esque should balance two seemingly opposing characteristics:

- Mysterious enough to create intrigue.
- Informative enough that customers understand what they are buying.

The brand should feel like an emerging fashion house that already understands its identity, rather than an amateur clothing startup.

Scarcity should be visible and meaningful but not manufactured to an obnoxious degree.

## 7. Core Commerce Model

Esque operates using a drop model.

Instead of maintaining the same permanent collection indefinitely:

- Esque launches a collection.
- The collection contains a limited selection of products.
- Products remain purchasable while inventory exists.
- Sold-out products remain visually accessible.
- Once the drop has ended, the collection enters the Archive.
- Esque introduces the next collection.

Every collection may have a different visual identity while remaining within the Esque design system.

## 10. Collections vs Categories

These concepts must remain separate.

### Category

Describes what the item is.

Example: Tops → Hoodies

### Collection

A product therefore belongs to both:

- Category: Tops / Hoodie

## 12. Entry Experience

Every new visitor encounters the Esque Access Gate.

This should not resemble a basic password form.

It functions as the first chapter of the Esque experience.

The objective is to create curiosity without making access frustrating.

## 13. Access Gate Design

The access page should contain:

- Esque logo
- password field
- Enter Esque CTA
- Request Access CTA
- subtle explanation of Esque
- animated fashion imagery
- product imagery moving through the environment
- model footage when production assets become available
- interactive cursor effects
- typography animation
- subtle forest-green accent
- responsive motion

The page should immediately communicate that Esque is a fashion brand.

## 14. Access Gate Motion Concept

Rather than a static background, products and visual fragments may move through the screen.

Potential sequence:

1. Black screen.
2. ESQUE typography enters.
3. Garment image crosses behind the typography.
4. Additional garments move at different depths.
5. User cursor subtly affects their movement.
6. Access interface resolves into view.

The result should feel like entering an editorial campaign rather than unlocking a traditional website.

Motion must remain quick.

The entrance sequence should never become an obstacle for returning users.

## 15. Password Architecture

Esque will eventually support three conceptual levels.

### General Access

GENERAL ACCESS → standard collection

One universal password. Characteristics:

- reusable
- does not expire
- unlimited attempts
- easy to obtain
- used primarily as part of the brand experience

### Esque Private

ESQUE PRIVATE → private collection

Reserved for future development.

### VIP Access

VIP ACCESS → early drops

Used for future early-access programs.

## 16. Drop-Specific Early Access

Future drops may introduce a temporary early-access password.

Example:

- Public release: Friday — 12:00 PM
- Email members: Thursday — 12:00 PM

Email subscribers receive a drop-specific password allowing them to enter the new collection before general release.

After the early-access period concludes, normal access becomes available.

This creates meaningful email value without making Esque difficult to access.

## 17. Incorrect Password Experience

Incorrect passwords should not display a generic:

> Incorrect password.

Instead, Esque should use branded microcopy.

Examples:

- NOT THIS ONE.
- ACCESS NOT RECOGNIZED.
- TRY ANOTHER.
- or rotating messages.

The interaction can include:

- brief text distortion
- subtle typography displacement
- short green flash
- interface shake of only a few pixels

The response should be playful and mysterious rather than punitive.

Unlimited attempts are allowed.

## 18. Access Persistence

Recommended behavior:

After successfully entering Esque, store access on the visitor's browser for approximately 30 days.

This preserves the gate as part of discovering Esque without forcing loyal visitors to enter the same password every time.

A visitor can still manually log out of access if necessary.

Drop-specific VIP access is stored separately.

## 19. Request Access

Selecting Request Access opens a minimal interface requesting:

- First name
- Email
- Marketing consent

A clear consent checkbox must be provided.

Submission performs two functions:

1. Adds the visitor to the Esque marketing list.
2. Immediately delivers the current access password.

## 27. Interactive Model — Desktop

A full model image displays an Esque outfit.

Individual garments have invisible hotspot regions.

Example: HOODIE, PANTS, JEWELRY

When the cursor enters the hoodie region:

- Hoodie becomes visually emphasized.
- Other garments and surrounding imagery darken.
- Selected garment receives a subtle glow/light treatment.
- Product information animates into view.

Information includes:

- product name
- category
- short description
- available colors
- available sizes
- View Product
- Add to Bag

Price may appear here despite general homepage price suppression because the visitor has deliberately interacted with the product.

Alternatively, price may remain hidden until product/quick-add interaction during early design testing.

## 28. Interactive Model — Selection

Selecting the garment transitions the visitor into its product page.

The preferred transition is a shared visual transition.

Example:

1. User clicks hoodie.
2. The visual area surrounding the hoodie enlarges.
3. The model image transforms toward the product detail layout.
4. The product page resolves around it.

Avoid a traditional hard page refresh whenever technically reasonable.

## 29. Shop Entire Look

The model experience contains: SHOP THE LOOK

Selecting it exposes every product being worn.

The user may:

- select individual pieces
- choose size/color for each
- add selected pieces
- add the complete outfit

The system must never add undefined variants automatically.

All required size/color selections must be resolved first.

## 30. Interactive Model — Mobile

Hover does not exist on touch devices.

Mobile behavior:

1. User sees the model.
2. Interactive garment regions receive subtle visual cues.
3. User taps a garment.
4. Other garments darken.
5. Selected item is highlighted.
6. Product information appears.
7. Second action opens product or adds it to bag.

The system must make discoverability obvious enough that users understand the image is interactive.

> Custom headless storefront + Shopify commerce backend

Shopify manages:

- products
- variants
- pricing
- inventory
- collections
- customer records
- orders
- discounts
- payment processing
- checkout
- fulfillment data
- refunds

The custom Esque frontend controls:

- design
- animation
- navigation
- page transitions
- product discovery
- interactive model
- collection experiences
- typography
- access gate
- frontend search experience

This gives Esque Shopify's mature commerce infrastructure without forcing Esque to look like a Shopify template.

## 51. Frontend Architecture

Recommended: React-based headless application with Shopify Storefront APIs / Shopify's headless tooling.

Shopify's Hydrogen ecosystem is designed specifically for headless storefronts and provides commerce primitives, Storefront API integrations, cart capabilities, analytics and Shopify checkout integration.

The implementation should avoid coupling Esque so tightly to experimental framework features that upgrading becomes difficult.

## 52. Why Not Stripe-Only

Stripe is an excellent payments platform.

However, using Stripe alone would require Esque to build or integrate additional systems for:

- product management
- variants
- inventory
- order management
- discounts
- customer commerce history
- fulfillment
- returns
- merchandising
- commerce analytics

For Esque V1 this creates unnecessary engineering work.

Stripe may remain relevant underneath or alongside future infrastructure, but Shopify should be the commerce platform.

## 62. Motion Philosophy

Esque should use fast responsive motion, not slow theatrical animation everywhere.

Motion exists to:

- explain hierarchy
- reinforce interaction
- create continuity
- establish brand identity
- produce surprise
- increase perceived quality

Motion must not obstruct shopping.

## 63. Motion Intensity

Target: approximately 7/10 visually, but approximately 3/10 interaction complexity.

In other words: The site should look sophisticated while remaining simple to operate.

## 64. Motion System

Recommended motion language:

**Microinteraction** — 100–250 ms. Used for: buttons, hover, icons, navigation states.

**UI Transitions** — 250–450 ms. Used for: menus, filters, quick add, product states.

**Editorial Transitions** — 400–800 ms. Used selectively for: collection entrances, major image transitions, homepage scenes.

Avoid routine animations lasting multiple seconds.

## 65. Approved Interaction Techniques

Use selectively:

- image masks
- clip reveals
- subtle scale
- parallax
- cursor proximity
- magnetic interactions
- layout morphing
- shared-element page transitions
- drag interactions
- inertial movement
- animated typography
- image displacement
- depth transforms
- subtle WebGL effects where justified

## 66. Interaction Rule

Every major animation must satisfy at least one of these:

1. Improve comprehension.
2. Communicate state.
3. Connect two interface states.
4. Reinforce Esque's identity.
5. Produce meaningful delight.

If it satisfies none of these, remove it.

## 67. Cursor

Desktop may use a subtle custom cursor.

Default state: small restrained indicator.

Contextual states may display: VIEW, DRAG, SHOP, OPEN, NEXT

The cursor may expand over interactive imagery.

Do not replace normal interaction cues so aggressively that buttons become ambiguous.

## 68. Magnetic Effects

Selective controls may respond slightly to cursor proximity.

Good candidates:

- hero CTA
- View Product
- menu items
- collection navigation

Movement should remain restrained. Buttons should not visibly escape the user's cursor.

## 69. Page Transitions

Navigation should feel continuous.

Examples:

- Collection card → Collection page
- Product card → Product page
- Interactive garment → Product page

Preferred technique: shared imagery / typography transitions.

However, transitions must degrade gracefully and must never prevent navigation.

## 70. Drag Interactions

Drag should be used only where users naturally understand it.

Potential uses:

- campaign gallery
- product media carousel
- collection editorial strip
- future lookbook

Always provide a non-drag alternative.

## 71. No Audio

The Esque website will not use:

- background music
- interaction sounds
- transition sounds

All sensory identity comes from: motion, imagery, typography, spacing, color.

## 72. Mobile Philosophy

Mobile is not a reduced desktop website.

It should feel intentionally designed for touch.

Priority: fast, visual, thumb-friendly, responsive, app-like where beneficial.

Desktop hover interactions translate into tap interactions.

Drag becomes swipe.

Navigation should remain highly discoverable.

## 73. Mobile Navigation

Recommended: Full-screen menu triggered from the top navigation.

Avoid an overloaded permanent bottom navigation because the website is primarily a fashion experience rather than an application.

Bag state should remain immediately accessible.

## 74. Mobile Product Cards

Default: strong imagery, product name.

Tap product: opens PDP.

Quick Add remains accessible through a clear secondary action.

No interaction should depend solely on hover.

## 75. Performance

Performance is a core product requirement.

Esque may sacrifice a small amount of raw speed for significant visual improvement, but unnecessary performance cost is unacceptable.

Priority order:

1. Usability
2. Perceived speed
3. Visual quality
4. Animation sophistication

## 76. Performance Requirements

The implementation should use:

- responsive images
- next-generation image formats
- intelligent video loading
- lazy loading
- route-level code splitting
- edge/server rendering where useful
- caching
- prefetching
- optimized fonts
- reduced unnecessary JavaScript
- GPU-friendly transforms
- animation cleanup
- selective WebGL loading

Heavy visual modules should load only when needed.

## 77. Progressive Enhancement

- High-powered device: full experience.
- Low-powered device: simplified effects.
- Reduced-motion setting: reduced animation.
- Weak network: optimized image quality and progressive content.

The ability to shop must never depend on advanced animation support.

## 78. Accessibility

Target: WCAG 2.2 AA wherever practical.

Requirements include:

- semantic markup
- proper labels
- screen-reader support
- keyboard navigation
- visible focus states
- sufficient contrast
- reduced-motion support
- alternative text
- accessible forms
- accessible dialogs
- appropriate touch targets

Accessibility and luxury design are not competing objectives.

## 79. SEO

Esque and its products should be searchable through Google and other search engines.

Requirements:

- indexable product pages
- indexable collection pages
- descriptive metadata
- canonical URLs
- structured product data
- XML sitemap
- Open Graph data
- optimized image metadata
- crawlable site architecture

## 80. Important Access-Gate SEO Rule

The password experience must not prevent search engines from discovering the product catalog.

The gate should therefore function primarily as a visitor experience rather than a server-level wall blocking every product route from crawlers.

SEO-visible pages and structured metadata need to remain discoverable.

Users entering product pages through Google may still be introduced to Esque access behavior intelligently without destroying organic search usability.

This needs special implementation attention.

**Commerce** — Revenue, Orders, Average order value, Conversion rate, Cart abandonment, Checkout abandonment, Product conversion, Refunds, returning customers

**Product Behavior** — Product views, Add to Bag, Quick Add usage, Wishlist adds, variant selection, sold-out views, back-in-stock requests

**Engagement** — returning visitors, pages per session, collection engagement, scroll completion, major CTA engagement

## 93. Design North Star

Every design decision should be evaluated against five questions.

1. **Is it Esque?** Does it feel niche, premium and innovative?
2. **Is it memorable?** Will the visitor remember the experience?
3. **Is it understandable?** Can the visitor immediately determine what to do?
4. **Is it useful?** Does the interaction improve the experience rather than simply demonstrate animation?
5. **Is it fast?** Does the interaction preserve responsiveness?

If an idea is visually impressive but fails usability, simplify it.

If an idea is usable but completely generic, redesign it.

The target lies between those extremes.

## 102. Final Experience Definition

Esque should not resemble a Shopify theme with animations attached.

The intended experience is:

> A digital fashion house powered by an ecommerce system underneath it.

The visitor enters through a branded access experience.

They encounter an editorial collection rather than a wall of products.

Typography establishes identity.

Photography establishes desire.

Motion establishes quality.

Interactive models allow users to discover individual garments.

Experimental navigation creates memorability.

Product discovery becomes playful.

Once the customer decides to purchase, complexity disappears.

Variant selection becomes clear.

Bag behavior becomes familiar.

Checkout becomes conventional and trusted.

That contrast is important:

> Experimental while exploring. Effortless while buying.

That should become the defining UX philosophy of Esque.
